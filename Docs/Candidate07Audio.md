# Candidate07 recorded-audio integration

Source credits, licenses, raw recordings, editable recipes and exact generated hashes are in [ArtSource/Audio/Candidate07](../ArtSource/Audio/Candidate07/README.md). The source bank has 134 cues; offline generation and exact recomputation pass. Engine import/build, packaged behavior and perceptual listening are separate verification stages and are not asserted by this implementation note.

Weapon reports keep the existing discharge event and asset selection. Reload, pump, extraction and dry-fire cues use the existing operation events and timestamps. The catalog changes audio references only. Upgraded shots combine recorded physical reports with the existing intentional energy formula; machine and pickup cues remain unchanged.

Infected effort is supplied by the infected animation graph's once-per-action marker. Foot contacts come from its evaluated contact callbacks; player footsteps are isolated in `UONE07FootstepComponent`, after the player's evaluated pose. The player component arms each foot when lifted above 9 cm and emits only on a descending crossing to 6 cm or less with actual ground support, grounded movement and a per-foot 0.18 s cooldown. Stationary, airborne and dead players reset that contact history. No independent step timer drives either graph.

Both owners trace the evaluated toe/foot against actual world support, requesting physical-material data. `SurfaceType1` or the explicit `ONE_AudioMetal` actor/component tag selects metal; other surfaces use the concrete bank. This is a two-surface implementation, not a promise of arbitrary automatic material recognition. Anatomy retains the established source convention: `_r` bones are the infected's left side.

Creature voices have two reusable components per actor. Their positions follow the physical mesh head, or spine after decapitation, including falls and corpses. Existing shared concurrency stays at four breathing voices, seven action voices and three sparse environment voices. Breathing remains staggered and yields to actions; death cancels living sounds, and shutdown stops the actor's voices. Fall effort and floor impact are separate events. A contact impulse does not generate a fall voice unless production fall logic requests it.

The shared Foley pool allocates at most 12 reusable components and has a 12-voice concurrency group. Body/flesh, footsteps, bullet/environment contacts, magazines and casings share that budget. Contacts use spatial attenuation (80 cm inner radius, 1,100 cm falloff), fixed pitch, and event-specific gain/priority. At capacity a lower-priority arrival is rejected; otherwise the lowest-priority eligible voice, then the quietest at that priority, is reused. Quiet casing chatter cannot evict a higher-priority body/player contact. Counts describe submitted events/components and do not prove every request was audible.

Physical case and magazine contacts read the incoming normal speed from the existing flight sweep, with no physics or lifetime changes. A minimum 65 cm/s rejects quiet settling. First contact has full event gain; later permitted bounces use 35%, a 0.14 s case / 0.16 s magazine cooldown, and at most three cues per actor. Infected body contacts use a 0.18 s cooldown and the same 65 cm/s threshold, with gain scaled by normal contact speed. Gore-piece callers must supply their own contact cooldown. Destruction/cleanup does not manufacture impact events. Game over rejects further shared Foley; lifecycle cleanup stops active contacts.

The existing cvars `one.Audio.Weapons` (1), `one.Audio.Zombies` (0.8), `one.Audio.Ambience` (0.55) remain. `one.Audio.Foley` (0.8) controls contact/footstep gain; all clamp to 0–2. `one.Audio.ContactReverb` defaults to 0.12 and clamps to 0–0.4. This is a send to active scene reverb, not synthesized room acoustics or evidence that a room effect was heard. The accepted pawn-proximity listener remains unchanged.

The source pool includes documented analogues: AR-15/M4-family rather than verified exact M4A1, Nova reports and SXP mechanisms for 870, object Foley for magazines/bullet impacts, wet towel for body/flesh, and mini-fan exhaust for facility machinery. Four excerpts/two mic perspectives plus two edits form each six-variant firearm bank; some mic views may share the same performances. Such limits must survive final reporting. Numerical headroom checks do not replace audition or establish that performed vocal cuts suit their provisional gameplay roles.
