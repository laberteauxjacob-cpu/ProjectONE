# Candidate05 S4 motion and stride visual review

Source: `6b8621cef9d2a87de6e6eabc1743359c6274da5a`. Capture: `20260909_181512_75878284`.

All sixteen directional stride windows and twelve motion windows were inspected chronologically through 59 native-scale lossless crop sheets covering 567 distinct captured JPEGs. Thirty-two selected originals were also viewed at their full 1600x900 size. Two additional corpse sheets reframe the same 22 rows because the default crops lost the target.

Result: selected windows reviewed with limitations. This is an agent visual review, not user approval of the visual direction. The separate automated motion fixture passed 80 checks with zero failures and captured 1,389 frames.

Directional strides: All eight walk and eight run windows show changing, alternating leg and foot poses across consecutive capture rows. The upper body keeps its weapon aimed while travel directions change. Run windows show wider/faster leg-phase changes and faster floor-grid passage than the walk samples. The gait still looks mechanical: knee and foot changes are sharp, legs cross or nearly overlap in side-facing views, and the upper aim pose remains rigid. No persistent gross whole-leg floor penetration is visible in these selected views. This does not establish exact world-space planted-foot stability, all start/stop transitions, or polished natural human weight.

Turns: The gun and torso move through intermediate orientations in both turn directions. The feet stay close and fairly quiet through part of each turn, then make a brief following step/crossing change as the body turns. The stationary base through part of the turn and the quick following foot changes remain visible. These sheets do not establish smooth continuous weight transfer or exact planting.

Pivots: Each pivot shows a sharp upper-body and weapon reversal between adjacent captured rows, followed by a short foot/cross-step change and settling. Aim changes are visibly prompt, but the transition reads as an abrupt pose change. It should not be described as a silky or fully weighted turn, or as a measurement of native mouse responsiveness.

Attack families: All three explicit family windows show raised-arm preparation, knee bend/forward step, torso incline, reach/contact and recovery. Red directional damage feedback and blood appear near the contact beat. The two-handed family has paired forearms raised together and a clearer different silhouette; swipe and rake remain similar at this overhead angle, with the rake arc held higher near the head for longer. These explicit standstill family fixtures do not establish ordinary crowded approach or the complete run-to-strike transition. A short player upper-body reaction is visible, but static sheets cannot establish whether controls feel interrupted. Exact contact counts and health changes are automated evidence, separate from this visual judgment.

Live hits: Muzzle flash/tracer, cyan impact marker and localized red spray are visible on successive shots while the target remains upright and continues approaching. The marker fades and refreshes across the sequence. A detached orange forearm falls to the floor while the target continues moving. The body and joint movement remains angular. This view does not isolate subtle hit flinch from locomotion and limb separation, and does not determine exact hit region.

Kill fall: The cyan marker is followed by a gold/yellow kill marker. The target moves from standing through a knee drop and torso fold to a curled position on the floor as the player retreats. Blood and a separated arm remain visible nearby. The gold marker fades before the later corpse shot. This is one visible death sequence at the fixture camera angle; it does not establish broad death variety or physical realism.

Corpse: Corrected crops show the downed curled body and separated head/arm while the player retreats. Frame1334 visibly has muzzle flash and a tracer reaching the corpse, followed by a small fresh red spray. The crosshair remains plain white with no renewed gold kill marker through frame1344, while the corpse remains down. The original two corpse crops exclude most of the target; those pages alone cannot support corpse findings. The same22 original rows were reviewed in new lossless crops covering the target. No additional live scene, artificial frame or retouch was introduced.

Review limitations:

- Chronological inspection means reading each selected sheet left-to-right/top-to-bottom and then successive pages. It is not continuous video playback or real-time motion/audition.
- 567 distinct selected original JPEGs were inspected through59 native-scale lossless PNG crop sheets;32 of those JPEGs were also opened in their original1600x900 full-frame form.
- Selected steady stride windows do not cover every transition, initial camera settling or every rendered engine frame. Capture callbacks sample the render stream.
- Scripted offscreen fixture images cannot establish native OS keyboard/mouse feel, display latency, performance approval, host isolation or a matched Candidate04 comparison.
- No audio was auditioned during this review. Automated cue, WAV, timing, collision and damage assertions are independent evidence.
- One visual review of selected samples does not establish user acceptance of the Candidate05 visual direction or that all gameplay animation polish is complete.
